# Visual treatment (Designer skill)

The house treatment, learned from the owner's corrections:

- The photo fills the frame. The text zone is GROWN out of the
  photo: its lower part darkens gradually into the brand's main
  colour. Never paste a picture onto a flat band, and never
  crop the subject away to make room for words.
- Type sits in tiers: a small rule and kicker, the headline,
  an optional sub-line in the accent colour, then the call to
  action. Type carries a soft shadow over the darkened zone.
- Plates get a light grade toward the brand palette and a soft
  vignette before compositing.
- Concept posters carry ONE visual metaphor, drawn literally
  (messages are chat bubbles with tails, never abstract orbs),
  and must pass two checks: the image never contradicts its
  one-line claim, and a stranger gets it in one second.
- Carousels: the cover is full-bleed and may carry a feeling
  the headline pays off; story slides keep the whole scene
  visible with the words in the grown zone below it.
