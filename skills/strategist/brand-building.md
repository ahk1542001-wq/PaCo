# Brand building, level by level

A brand is built in five levels, in order, and a level is only
real when the owner has approved it. Drafts are proposals;
approval is the fact. Levels stay provisional until research
validates them, and the workflow says so rather than declaring
a finished brand.

1. Identity: the name, the palette with roles, the logo and
   its usage rule, the brand font. Decided through design work
   and recorded as data; this level is approved when the data
   exists, not drafted as prose.
2. Audience: who the brand is for, in one profile: who they
   are, the pains as lived moments (the message unanswered at
   midnight, not "communication challenges"), how they buy,
   and who is explicitly not the audience.
3. Positioning: one sentence that says what the brand is and
   how it is different, plus a bio line, the Messenger greeting
   and three messages the brand repeats until they are known.
   Draft three options; the owner picks.
4. Offer: the service menu, one service per line, in the words
   a buyer uses. The menu is internal data; public copy never
   carries prices.
5. Journey: the path from first sight to loyal client, as
   named steps with what happens at each: See, Message, free
   mini-audit, Quote, Build, Monthly Care, and the proof loop
   back to See.

Drafting rules: write from the business brief and the approved
levels below, never invent facts the brief does not carry, and
keep each level's text in the shape its brand-data key expects
(audience and positioning and journey as prose, the offer as
one service per line).
